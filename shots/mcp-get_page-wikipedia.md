# Ghost

> Wikipedia article · en.wikipedia.org

In [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore "Folklore"), a **ghost** is the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul "Soul") or [spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(supernatural_entity\) "Spirit (supernatural entity)") of a dead [person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") or non-human [animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal") that is believed by some people to be able to appear to the living. In [ghostlore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostlore "Ghostlore"), descriptions of ghosts vary widely, from an invisible presence to translucent or barely visible wispy shapes to realistic, lifelike forms, whether they resemble humans or animals. The deliberate attempt to contact the spirit of a deceased person is known as [necromancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necromancy "Necromancy"), or in [spiritism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardecist_spiritism "Kardecist spiritism") as a _[séance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séance "Séance")_. Other terms associated with it are apparition, haunt, haint, phantom, [poltergeist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist "Poltergeist"), [shade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shade_\(mythology\) "Shade (mythology)"), specter, spirit, spook, wraith, [demon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon "Demon"), and [ghoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghoul "Ghoul").

The belief in the existence of an [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife"), as well as manifestations of the spirits of the dead, is widespread, dating back to [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism") or [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_worship "Ancestor worship") in pre-literate cultures. Certain religious practices—funeral rites, [exorcisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exorcism "Exorcism"), and some practices of [spiritualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_\(beliefs\) "Spiritualism (beliefs)") and [ritual magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual_magic "Ritual magic")—are specifically designed to rest the spirits of the dead. Ghosts are generally described as solitary, human-like essences, though stories of ghostly armies and the ghosts of animals other than humans have also been recounted. They are believed to haunt particular [locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reportedly_haunted_locations "List of reportedly haunted locations"), objects, or people they were associated with in life. According to a 2009 study by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center"), 18% of Americans say they have seen a ghost.

Ghosts are not a scientifically testable concept. The existence of ghosts is impossible to [falsify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsifiability "Falsifiability"), and [ghost hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_hunting "Ghost hunting") has been classified as [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience"). Despite centuries of investigation, there is no [scientific evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_evidence "Scientific evidence") that any location is inhabited by the spirits of the dead. Historically, certain toxic and [psychoactive plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive_plant "Psychoactive plant") (such as _[datura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datura "Datura")_ and _[hyoscyamus niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoscyamus_niger "Hyoscyamus niger")_), whose use has long been associated with necromancy and the [underworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underworld "Underworld"), have been shown to contain [anticholinergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticholinergic "Anticholinergic") compounds that are pharmacologically linked to [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia "Dementia") (specifically [DLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia_with_Lewy_bodies "Dementia with Lewy bodies")) as well as histological patterns of [neurodegeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodegeneration "Neurodegeneration"). Recent research has indicated that ghost sightings may be related to degenerative brain diseases such as [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease"). Common prescription medication and [over-the-counter drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-counter_drugs "Over-the-counter drugs") (such as [sleep aids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_aids "Sleep aids")) may also, in rare instances, cause ghost-like hallucinations, particularly [zolpidem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolpidem "Zolpidem") and [diphenhydramine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diphenhydramine "Diphenhydramine"). Older reports linked [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide "Carbon monoxide") poisoning to ghost-like hallucinations.

In [folklore studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_studies "Folklore studies"), ghosts fall within the [motif index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motif-Index_of_Folk-Literature "Motif-Index of Folk-Literature") designation E200–E599 ("Ghosts and other revenants").

## Terminology

### Etymology

The English word [_ghost_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ghost "wikt:ghost") comes from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English "Old English") _[gāst](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gast#Old_English "wikt:gast")_ ("breath, spirit, soul, ghost"), which can be traced back to [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic "Proto-Germanic") _[\*gaistaz](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/gaistaz "wikt:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/gaistaz")_ ("spirit, ghost").
